# Hackathon Winning Strategy

## Judging alignment

The brief scores technological implementation, complete/coherent design, credible impact, and quality of idea. LookWeave should make one causal loop undeniable rather than showing a long feature tour.

## Three-minute demo

### 0:00–0:20 — The tension

Open on the “Quiet structure” look. Say: “It looks right. But can I wear it independently?” Show the button blazer’s confirmed 42-second interaction and two retries.

### 0:20–0:55 — Observe, do not diagnose

Open Wearability Lab, choose the blazer and fastening task, run a short real observation, and let the person confirm how it felt. Emphasize that LookWeave learns the garment interaction, not a disability label.

### 0:55–1:30 — Prove the engineering

Generate the look with easy one-hand closure enabled. Show the solver replacing only the blazer, preserving four pieces, and cite the garment evidence. State clearly that the LLM can interpret a request, but cannot override confirmed restrictions.

### 1:30–2:05 — YouCam earns its place

Open Virtual Studio and run the real YouCam task. The visual question is whether the easier alternative still preserves the person’s identity and intended silhouette. Explain that VTO supports the appearance decision while wearability comes from confirmed evidence.

### 2:05–2:35 — Close the learning loop

Ask by voice, “Why did you change the jacket?” The agent reads the observation, product facts, and minimum-change result. Then request a more casual direction and show the solver checking it again.

### 2:35–3:00 — Land the thesis

Show the Living Wearability Profile and end with:

> Most fashion AI knows what looks good. LookWeave learns what actually works for you.

## What judges should remember

- Direct evidence from a real garment interaction
- Inclusion without diagnosis or stereotypes
- A hard-constraint engine, not an unconstrained chatbot
- One exact, explainable change instead of regenerating everything
- YouCam used at the decisive confidence moment
- A complete, beautiful consumer product

## Demo safety checklist

- Use a real YouCam response in the recorded submission.
- Record on the target device and keep a prevalidated second input ready.
- Do not claim a closure force, fit outcome, or accessibility property that was not observed or documented.
- If demonstrating lived disability experience, involve and credit a person with that experience and obtain consent.
- Show failure recovery once during rehearsal, but keep the final video within three minutes.
- Avoid copyrighted music, third-party marks, and retailer logos.
